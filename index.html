Unofficial peer guide UNSW PhD students

NeurIPS 2026 is in Sydney. Here is how to go, and what to submit to.

The fortieth NeurIPS runs at ICC Sydney from 6 to 12 December 2026. If you are based in Sydney, your travel and accommodation costs are zero and registration is the only real barrier, which makes this the cheapest NeurIPS you will ever attend. This page collects the routes to attending free, and every Sydney workshop with its actual deadline.

Where and when

Three cities, one conference

Sydney 6 to 12 Dec 2026 Main venue, ICC Sydney. Workshops run 11 to 12 December.
Atlanta 9 to 13 Dec 2026 Satellite. Workshops 12 to 13 December. Requires travel.
Paris 9 to 13 Dec 2026 Satellite. Workshops 12 to 13 December. Requires travel.

Check the city before you get excited. The 109 accepted workshops are split across all three sites, and a workshop's own website often does not make its city obvious. Only the 54 below are in Sydney. The other 55 need a flight.

The conference programme is confirmed: Expo talks on Sunday 6 December, tutorials on Monday 7 December, main sessions Tuesday 8 to Thursday 10 December, and workshops on Friday 11 and Saturday 12 December. A virtual pass covers the whole week.

Registration pricing for 2026 was not published as of 22 July 2026. The page said pricing is not yet available. A widely quoted student rate of about USD 375 comes from 2023 and is indicative only, so do not budget on it.

How to attend cheaply or free as a Sydney local

Ranked for someone who lives here. Being local changes the maths: hotel support is worth nothing to you, so routes that bundle accommodation rank below routes that simply cover registration.

  1. Student volunteer Dates expected

    The strongest route, because it does not depend on having an accepted paper or on anyone's budget. You work two shifts of roughly four hours each at the venue, and your registration is refunded once the shifts are done.

    The catch is cash flow, not eligibility. Registration is paid upfront and refunded afterwards, so you need the money available in early December. Volunteering is offered at the main venue only, which for 2026 means Sydney. Based on 2025, applications open around 1 September and close around 1 October AoE, with decisions by about 10 October.

  2. Financial assistance Terms expected

    A separate NeurIPS program awarding complimentary registration and/or a prepaid hotel stay of up to seven nights. In 2025 it explicitly prioritised student and junior postdoc conference authors.

    For a local, the hotel half is worthless, so the realistic benefit is the same free registration the volunteer route gives you. It ranks second because its priority ordering only favours you if you have an accepted paper. In 2025 it opened 1 September and closed 2 October AoE.

  3. Apply for both, but you can only win one

    The 2025 terms stated that you may apply for both the grant and the volunteer program, but you will not be selected for both. Author notifications land on 24 September 2026, a confirmed date, which falls before the expected 1 October volunteer deadline. You will know your author status in time to choose.

  4. Supervisor or school funds

    A direct request for registration only, with no travel or accommodation attached, which is a much smaller ask than the usual conference request.

    Worth knowing: UNSW schemes such as PRSS and DRTG are oriented toward travel and toward presenting your own work, so a local no-travel registration may fit them awkwardly. A supervisor's grant or discretionary school funds are often the more natural fit. Have the conversation early rather than in October.

  5. Affinity group support

    Groups including Women in Machine Learning, Queer in AI and Black in AI have historically run their own registration support, funded by their sponsors and applied for separately from the main NeurIPS programs.

    Eligibility depends on group membership and the schemes are smaller and announced later, so treat this as a parallel long shot rather than a plan. Several of these groups also run their own workshops, listed in the Sydney table below.

  6. Top reviewer awards

    NeurIPS has historically given free registration to its highest rated reviewers. It ranks last because you cannot apply for it, the 2026 reviewing round is already well advanced, and any award arrives too late to plan around. If it lands, it simply displaces whichever route above it worked.

54 workshops, Sydney

Sydney workshops and their real deadlines

Sorted by deadline, soonest first. Sydney workshops run on 11 and 12 December 2026 at ICC Sydney. Most sites say "11 or 12 December, exact day to be confirmed", because NeurIPS has not published the day by day assignment yet.

Closing before 29 August, in deadline order

Ten Sydney workshops have something due before 29 August. Eight close entirely, and two more, ASCI and AutoMLR, take an early abstract before a 29 August final paper. The pair on 26 August are the easiest to miss, because they sit just three days ahead of the date most people have in their heads.

  • 10 Aug Africa in AI, MusIML and Queer in AI, early visa support rounds. Each has a later main deadline, but this round is the one that helps with travel paperwork.
  • 20 Aug LatinX in AI, and the ASCI abstract registration.
  • 22 Aug NeurReps, and the AutoMLR abstract.
  • 26 Aug 8th Robot Learning Workshop and Physical Understanding for Decision-Making. Note the second closes at 08:00 UTC, not at end of day AoE, so it is effectively an evening deadline in Sydney.
  • 28 Aug Interpretability as a Science.

Why so many land on 29 August

29 August 2026 AoE is NeurIPS's own suggested submission date for workshop contributions, published in the Call for Workshops. It is a suggestion, not a rule. Of the 54 workshops here, 34 adopted it exactly, 8 close earlier, 9 close later, and 3 have published nothing yet. Some sites list "30 August 12:00 UTC", which is the same instant as 29 August 23:59 AoE, not a different day.
